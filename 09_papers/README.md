## Shared foundation

All four papers build on the unified theoretical and empirical infrastructure of the thesis:
- The identification hierarchy (thesis Chapter 5, Section "Identification hierarchy and claim structure")
- The five-channel real-options framework (thesis Chapter 3, Section "Generalised real-options framework: five channels of policy action")
- The seven-friction policy taxonomy (thesis Chapter 3, Section "Policy mechanism taxonomy")
- The mechanism-falsifiability framework (thesis Chapter 3, Section 3.7 + Appendix A.13)
- The 80+ analyses in `06_thesis_extensions/`

## Submission strategy

**Phase 1 (immediate)**: Paper 1 to JAE. Independent methodological contribution that does not require the other three papers to be in print first.

**Phase 2 (after Paper 1 review feedback)**: Papers 2 and 3 to Energy Economics and JEEM respectively. These papers cite Paper 1 for the TVP methodology and each other for the offtake-vs-carrot-policy comparative analysis.

**Phase 3 (after empirical papers established)**: Paper 4 to REEP. The theoretical framework benefits from being preceded by Papers 2 and 3 in the citation trail because the falsifiability framework's retrospective assessment draws on the empirical evidence from those papers.

## Connection to the dissertation

Each paper corresponds to a chapter of the final PhD dissertation in the sandwich-PhD format:
- Paper 1 → Chapter 6 (Results III: Time-Varying Carbon-Conditional Hazard)
- Paper 2 → Chapter 6 + Chapter 7 (Results I + Results II)
- Paper 3 → Chapter 8 (Results II: The Offtake-Commitment Mechanism)
- Paper 4 → Chapter 3 (Theoretical Framework)

The dissertation also contains integrative material that does not appear in any of the four papers: the introduction (Chapter 1), the literature review (Chapter 2), the data chapter (Chapter 4), the methodology chapter (Chapter 5), the counterfactual scenarios (Chapter 9), the discussion of methodological discipline (Chapter 10), the policy implications (Chapter 11), and the conclusion (Chapter 12).
