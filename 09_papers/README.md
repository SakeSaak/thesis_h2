## Shared foundation

All four papers build on the unified theoretical and empirical infrastructure of the thesis:
- The identification hierarchy (thesis Chapter 5 + Appendix A.12)
- The five-channel real-options framework (μ, σ, ρ, η, κ) (thesis Chapter 3)
- The seven-friction policy taxonomy (thesis Chapter 3)
- The mechanism-falsifiability framework (thesis Chapter 3.7 + Appendix A.13), explicitly framed as theoretically organising rather than empirically structurally separable
- The identification robustness battery (thesis Appendix A.14): three formal tests demonstrating that channel-attribution claims are empirically underdetermined in observational data
- The extension portfolio in `06_thesis_extensions/` (Bayesian methodology, state-space TVP, real-options calibration, modern DiD robustness, identification tests, counterfactual scenarios)

## Submission strategy

**Phase 1 (immediate)**: Paper 1 to *Journal of Applied Econometrics*. Independent methodological contribution that does not require the other three papers to be in print first.

**Phase 2 (after Paper 1 review feedback + thesis defence)**: Papers 2 and 3 to *Energy Economics* and *JEEM* respectively. These papers cite Paper 1 for the TVP methodology and each other for the offtake-vs-carrot-policy comparative analysis. Paper 2 also includes the IRA event-study corroboration of its principal 45Q estimate (Section 7.10).

**Phase 3 (after empirical papers established)**: Paper 4 to *REEP*. The theoretical framework benefits from being preceded by Papers 2 and 3 in the citation trail; Paper 4's empirical-identification note explicitly references the thesis Appendix A.14 evidence.
